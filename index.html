Problem

Problem Definition

People carrying physical wallets in crowded urban areas face a high risk of pickpocketing. When thefts happen, victims lose not only their money but also sensitive personal information.

Problem Motivation

Many people frequently travel through crowded public spaces such as airports, train stations, concerts, shopping centers, and tourist attractions where pickpocketing and theft are increasingly common. Losing a wallet can create serious financial and personal security issues. Victims may lose access to money, have their identities compromised, or spend significant time replacing important documents and canceling stolen cards. Existing solutions such as RFID-blocking wallets or Bluetooth trackers only partially address the problem. Most products either focus on preventing card skimming or helping recover lost items after theft has already occurred.

Existing Solutions

Anti-Theft Bags

  • What works well
    • Blocks card scanning (RFID protection)
    • Hidden zippers / compartments
    • Some are Slash-resistant
  • What is missing
    • Doesn't help recover the item
    • No real-time alert system
    • Can be bulky for everyday use

Apple AirTag

  • What works well
    • Real-time location tracking
    • Helps recover lost wallets
    • Compact and easy to carry
  • What is missing
    • Does not prevent theft from happening
    • Alerts may not trigger during pickpocketing
    • Tracker can be removed by the thief

Our Solution

Real-time Wallet Protection

A smart, biometric secured wallet that uses fingerprint authentication, motion detection, and wireless connectivity to prevent theft and protect your belongings in real time.

Testable Hypothesis

If a wallet combines biometric authentication, BLE-based proximity monitoring, and mobile alerts, then it can reduce successful pickpocketing incidents by preventing unauthorized access and notifying the owner immediately when separation occurs.

Technological Aspects of Solution

  • Layer 1 — The Face: The top shell features a precisely machined circular cutout for the fingerprint sensor. A small pinhole at the top houses the ambient light sensor, this allows the wallet to "know" it's in your pocket so it doesn't accidentally trigger.
  • Layer 2 — The Gesture Strip: Directly beneath the top shell is a capacitive touch module. It's designed to swipe or tap on the surface of the wallet to interact with it.
  • Layer 3 — The PCB: ESP32-S3 module, biometric sensor, power and connectivity management, audio feedback, motion sensors, LED indicators.
  • Layer 4 — The Battery: A rechargeable lithium battery.
  • Layer 5 — The Motor: A micro stepper motor positioned next to the battery, responsible for the card ejection mechanism.
  • Layer 6 — RFID Mesh: A layer that blocks contactless card skimming, protecting your cards from unauthorized RFID readers.
  • Layer 7 — The Card Stack: The compartment that holds your physical cards, secured behind all the protective layers above.
  • Layer 8 — The Chassis: The bottom shell that holds everything together.

What the Technology Will Do

  • Authenticate the owner via fingerprint before allowing the wallet to open
  • Detect unauthorized removal from your pocket using motion sensors
  • Send real-time alerts to your phone if potential theft is detected
  • Block RFID with a built-in shielding mesh
  • Provide haptic and audio feedback for user interactions

State Machine

PCB

The purpose of our PCB is for the fingerprint authentication so that only the registered owner(s) can open the wallet. Upon receiving the signal that the correct fingerprint was provided, the PCB will send a signal to the motor to unlock it. We also have an IMU to detect irregular motion in case of theft. Combined with a disconnected Bluetooth, the wallet will alarm the buzzer so that the owner can find it before the thief can get far.

Challenges

Unconnected Motor

Accidentally leaving the motor control unconnected was a big roadblock to overcome. We wired the motor control to the boot button control for the fix.

CAD Iterations

Constant iterations on the CAD model to make the measurements right as well as fitting it onto the product

NMOS Wired Incorrectly

When wiring the NMOS for the buzzer, we had switched the gate and source wirings on accident. The fix was to get a different nmos and manually bend the pins into their right spot.
